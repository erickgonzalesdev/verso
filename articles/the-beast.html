Field report · Classified until cleared · Team Delta

Something Large Moved

We were not sent to name it. We were sent to measure the gap between what the instruments reported and what the ground still claimed was empty — after the perimeter lights refused to dim, after the radio thinned to a carrier tone, after three sensors drew the same closed curve on maps that had never held one.

The brief called the sector quiet. On paper it was a calibration walk. In the field it was a city starting to forget itself: power still on, signage still upright, but street names no longer matched the grid.

The city did not fall in a single night. It leaked — first the trains, then the lights, then the names of streets. Field note · 01 · night cycle

The first trains failed to appear. The second arrived empty and never opened. By the third hour the platforms still filled, but no one agreed on which line they were waiting for. That is when the lights began to fail — not all at once, but in a pattern that later matched our curve.

From the ridge the city still looked intact. What was missing was the noise floor of a living district. In its place: a single continuous tone, as if the sector had been retuned to one frequency. We logged it at three stations with no decay by distance, then continued to the stairwell complex under orders not to name anything until instrument and terrain both failed as explanations.

What the instruments said

The first readings were dismissed as sensor noise. The second matched the first. The third anticipated: values rose seconds before we crossed the coordinates, as if the instruments were reading a map we had not yet walked onto. A circle on the paper grid — drawn only where the three sensors agreed — cut a survey cell still labeled empty ground.

  • Telemetry mismatched topography at three stations
  • Radio thinned after midnight; carrier tone persisted
  • Perimeter lights stayed day-locked through the night
  • Compass drift averaged +4.2° inside the closed curve

The stairwell was mid-century concrete and wrong in a way that took a minute to name. Flights did not land where the windows implied. Sound from below arrived late. We counted steps twice and got two totals without missing a tread. At successive landings the magnetometer pinned, released, then pinned on a different axis — with no ferrous mass in sight and handheld detectors quiet.

We marked landings with chalk and photographed each against a scale card. Later the chalk was still visible, but the scale cards no longer agreed with the laser rangefinder from the same moment. Either the photographs or the rangefinder was lying. Both had been calibrated that morning. Copy wraps under the plate so the float does not leave a blank column beside a tall frame.

Beyond the stairwell: cracked paving, dead grass, industrial lights still day-locked. In the middle, a pressure pattern in the dust — not footprints or tires, more like a weight lowered and lifted without sliding. We did not cross the center. Time, bearing, ambient sound: logged. No approach. No signal.

On the walk back the radio carrier dropped for seventeen seconds. Two members reported a voice without words they could place; the third heard nothing. The recorders held only the gap. We kept the notes boring on purpose so the section fills beside and under the plate without inventing a creature too early.

Compass and sky

Day four: continuous log, a route that should have returned us in under two hours. The compass and the sky disagreed before the first hour finished. The sun was where the almanac said; the needle was not. Agreement would have been comfort. Disagreement was data — and it preferred the sky. Rebuilt from solar bearing, the closed curve had grown east into the dry riverbed while the western edge held.

The riverbed held no water and almost no trash — swept, not cleaned. Cores at three points: two ordinary; one thin ash layer with no match in the municipal burn record. One detail held the ridge on telemetry while another walked the eastern edge until light failed, protocol on paper after the scale cards disagreed with the rangefinder.

Do not approach. Do not signal. Record time, bearing, and any change in ambient sound. If the figure resolves into equipment or weather, strike the entry. If it does not, file under beast until a better word arrives. Contact protocol · section 05

At the tree line something stood between the trunks long enough for three people to agree it was vertical and roughly human-scale, and not long enough for a fourth to raise a camera. Ambient sound dropped a few decibels, then returned. No one called out. No one stepped forward. Time, bearing, sound: logged. The entry was not struck.

We held twelve minutes. The magnetometer traced a slow open ellipse. When we withdrew it was because the protocol had already done its job: a record that did not need a creature to remain true. Night on the ridge was colder than the forecast. Day-locked lights stayed a pale rectangle against dark that should have swallowed them.

By morning the pressure pattern was gone — not weathered, not covered, gone. The closed curve had neither grown nor retreated. Continuous log, one unresolved protocol entry, a city that still would not match its maps. The calibration brief was finished; the obligation left was to write the gap so the next team would not rediscover it with their feet.

Extraction was uneventful. The road agreed with GPS until the last kilometer, where the track briefly preferred an older scar in the grass. We followed it until the main road reasserted itself. No one commented. Comment is how gaps become legends before the data is dry.

Prefer escapes rare

A span escape is still one section in the cycle: it widens the stage and advances the counter. Use it like a detour — only when the main road cannot carry the load. The field below is that detour.

From the wide ridge the sector looked almost ordinary again. Distance smooths disagreement into scenery. We trust the notebooks and the refusal to name what has not finished presenting itself. Same bones every time: frame, rails, one column, stage or crop, then kicker, title, credit. End of report. Team Delta stands down.

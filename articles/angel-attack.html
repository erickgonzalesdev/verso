Issue 01 · Sample · 12 min

The title card is the whole point of opening a piece. Black field, stacked series mark, episode number hard left, title hard right — the same grammar as the card that opens Neon Genesis Evangelion. Everything after that is just reading.

Body copy rides on concrete.css: no utility classes, a fixed measure, Helvetica, square list markers, and automatic dark mode from the OS. You write ordinary HTML — <p>, <blockquote>, <figure> — and the base stylesheet does the rest.

Structure comes from the size of the type and the silence around it, not from hairlines or uppercase chrome in the margins.

Hermes enters as structure, not as a colour. Centered wordmark, mono eyebrows with wide tracking, open feature blocks, monumental display — the same grammar as the Hermes site, drawn in black and white so the title card and the page share one field.

How to write a card

A title card is one block with three parts. Keep the series mark short enough to stack (three lines works; four is possible). Episode labels stay in mono and uppercase. The episode title sits opposite, flush right, same display face as the series. 

<header class="title-card">
  <p class="title-card__series">
    <span>Your</span>
    <span>Series</span>
    <span>Name</span>
  </p>
  <div class="title-card__meta">
    <p class="title-card__episode">Episode : 2</p>
    <p class="title-card__title">The Beast</p>
  </div>
</header>

What not to add

  • Utility frameworks on top of concrete — you will fight the measure.
  • Extra colour tokens scattered in components — keep them in theme.css.
  • A third “setting” before you have two real issues shipped.
